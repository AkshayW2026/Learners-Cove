CP Library · Databases

Start here

Five tools that cover most of what a CP student needs first. All open off campus with your school login.

Britannica School

Encyclopedia articles at different reading levels. Build context and collect vocabulary before you narrow your question.

Tip: note the key terms, people and events, then search specialised databases with them.

EBSCO Explora

Magazine and journal articles, newspapers and reference sources through one search box.

Tip: search a topic, then narrow by publication, date or source type.

JSTOR

Scholarly journal articles, books and primary sources. Strong on the humanities and social sciences, including ethics.

Tip: most useful once your question is focused.

Statista Campus License

Statistics, forecasts and reports on industries, markets and countries.

Tip: trace each statistic to its original source before using it as evidence.

The Economist

Weekly reporting and analysis of world affairs, business, science and culture.

Tip: good for context and analysis. Note the date, and remember it has its own point of view.

Beyond the usual databases

Sources that matter for an ethical dilemma

Your dilemma comes from your career area, so the best evidence often lives outside a standard database.

Codes of ethics & professional standards

Many professions publish codes of conduct. Look for the professional body in your field and read how it says its members should act.

Laws & regulations

What does the law require, and who regulates this field? Go to the regulator or government site itself.

Industry & company reports

Company and industry documents show what organisations say they do, and how they justify their choices.

Case studies & investigative reporting

Real cases make a dilemma concrete. Look for reported cases and news investigations, and read more than one account.

Scholarly ethics research

JSTOR and EBSCO Explora hold academic work on ethical theory and on ethics in specific fields.

Voices of those affected

A dilemma has stakeholders. Look for sources that speak for each one, including people with less power.

Where you found it is not what it is

JSTOR and EBSCO Explora are places you found a source. The source itself might be a journal article, a news report, a regulator’s report or a dataset. When you cite, say what the source is, who made it, and where you found it.

About Learners Cove

Learners Cove, the Academy library, sits at the centre of the Academy's nine-acre campus in the Financial District, overlooking the lake. Opened with the school in 2022, it serves readers from Early Years to Grade 10 across an IB continuum.

Our work is simple to state and harder to do: put the right resource in a young reader's hands at the moment they are curious, and teach them how to find the next one themselves.

Borrowing rules, copyright guidance and the acceptable-use policy are in the library handbook.
